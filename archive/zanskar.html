the second day

the whole trip turns on one pass.

2,050 m
manali · where it starts

You start at about 2,050 m in Manali and sleep the first night around 3,350 m at Keylong. That night is the acclimatisation — it is not padding.

Day two takes you over Shinkun La at roughly 5,091 m and down into Zanskar, then back up to camp at Gonbo Rangjon at around 4,300 m.

Almost all of it is done from a vehicle seat. The walking is short. The altitude is not.

before you apply

things we'd rather tell you now.

01 — the driving
Day two is a long one.

Zanskar is a long way in and the only way there is the road. Expect a full day in the vehicle on day two, and most of another on day three, on surfaces that go from smooth tarmac to nothing in the space of a kilometre. If long mountain drives make you miserable, this trip will too.

02 — the altitude
You cross 5,091 m and sleep near 4,300 m.

The Jispa night is deliberate acclimatisation. Altitude does not care how fit you are — if you feel wrong, you tell the trip leader immediately and we act on it. Oximeter checks each evening, and the vehicle means losing height fast is always an option.

03 — the camp
Tents at Gonbo Rangjon, not rooms.

One night in a hotel at Keylong, one night in shared tents at around 4,300 m: dry toilet tents, cold water, sub-zero after dark, and no network from the pass onwards. Camp is warm, fed and looked after. It is not a hotel and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

questions

Before you join.

How much of this is actually driving?+

Most of it. Day one is a half day to Keylong with stops at Solang, the Atal Tunnel and Sissu. Day two is the long one — Keylong over Shinkun La into Zanskar, Phuktal, and back out to camp. Day three brings you the whole way home. Come for the road and the landscape, not for a light schedule.

Do I need to trek anywhere?+

Only at Phuktal. The road ends short of the monastery and the last stretch is on foot — a short walk on a clear path, not a trek. No previous experience needed. If you can walk for half an hour on uneven ground at altitude, you’re fine.

Is 5,091 m safe if I’ve never been that high?+

For most people, yes — you cross the pass rather than sleep on it, and the Keylong night at 3,350 m is there to acclimatise you first. Camp at Gonbo Rangjon sits near 4,300 m, which is the part to respect. Drink far more water than feels natural, skip alcohol, and tell the leader the moment something feels off. We do oximeter checks each evening, and the vehicles mean we can drop height fast if we need to.

Why only October?+

Because that is when this road is at its best. The pass is open, the monsoon is done, the light is clean and the crowds have gone home. Earlier in the season the crossings are unpredictable; later, the pass shuts for winter.

Is there any phone network?+

Patchy at Keylong, nothing useful past the pass. Tell the people who worry about you before you leave Manali. The leader and local crew carry the emergency plan.

What exactly does the price cover?+

Two nights’ stay — hotel at Keylong, tents at Gonbo Rangjon — all the meals listed in the itinerary, every road transfer from Manali and back, camping equipment and kitchen, the local crew, an Unlistd trip leader, permits and all taxes. Applying costs nothing.

What if the pass closes?+

Weather makes that call, not us. If Shinkun La shuts we run the best version of the trip that Lahaul allows and tell you straight what changed. We don’t take chances with a 5,000 m crossing to keep an itinerary intact.

How does the selection work?+

Every application gets read by us, not sorted by a form. Twelve spots per batch, and they go to the first twelve who confirm once approvals go out. Applying early means you hear from us first.

Do you run this with other agencies?+

Never. Unlistd owns the full batch — the vehicles, the camp, the crew, the leader, all twelve spots. You will not be merged into someone else’s group at the bus stand.
